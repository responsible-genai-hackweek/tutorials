Landscape of tools

For coding specifically, there are five broad categories of AI agent tools.

IDE-native tools

e.g., GitHub Copilot in VS Code

Require no setup for someone already in that editor. Autocomplete plus chat. Lowest friction and lowest ceiling.

Command-line agents

e.g., Claude Code

Integrate with any workflow and require comfort at the terminal. Highest flexibility and steepest learning curve.

AI-native IDEs

e.g., Cursor, Windsurf

Purpose-built environments with deeper model integration. Middle ground on flexibility and capability ceiling.

Cloud-hosted assistants

e.g., ChatGPT, Claude.ai, Gemini

No local setup, but assume you will paste or upload what you want the agent to see. Least integration with your actual workflow.

Open-source options

e.g., OpenCode, Aider, continue.dev

Self-hostable and auditable. Often limited in model choice or capability. Maximum control over where your data goes.

The six-piece anatomy applies to all of them. Switching tools is a configuration exercise, not a re-learning exercise.
