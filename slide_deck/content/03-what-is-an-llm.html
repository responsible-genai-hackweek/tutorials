Large Language Models

A prediction machine,
not a knowledge base.

A large language model is a statistical model that predicts the next chunk of text given the text that came before it. At each step the model picks a likely next token (roughly three-quarters of a word) given everything that came before.

  • The model works in tokens. Everything about the model such as context limits, cost, speed is measured in tokens.
  • Randomness in this process is why you get different answers to the same question, and why the model can sound confident while being wrong.
  • The model has no memory, no internet access, and no knowledge beyond what it was trained on.
